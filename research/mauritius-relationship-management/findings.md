# Findings

## 1. Cross-Department Ownership and Handoffs

Financial-services complaints repeatedly involve delayed transfers, failure to
follow instructions, missing statements, documentation errors, unclear
communication, and slow insurance claims or payments. Customers repeatedly ask
for status, consuming staff time that could be spent resolving the case.

The Financial Services Ombudsperson began the reporting period with 696
outstanding matters, received 684, closed 985, and retained 395 outstanding.
Fifty-eight percent were settled between consumers and institutions.

The national Ombudsman reported 628 new complaints for 2024 and described
unimplemented recommendations, missing public records, inconsistent
administrative interpretations, and delays.

**Need:** one case history across channels and departments, explicit ownership,
next-update commitments, evidence, service levels, escalation, and closure
confirmation.

## 2. Employee Reward, Voice, Feedback, and Growth

The Business Mauritius National Employee Engagement Survey covered 21,603
respondents.

National results included:

- 52% considered total pay and benefits fair, the lowest-scoring area.
- 73% reported sufficient training opportunities.
- 75% reported development and growth opportunities.
- 76% expressed confidence in senior leadership.
- 76% said they could openly communicate views.
- 77% said different opinions were openly discussed.
- 79% reported regular constructive feedback.
- 81% reported good use of technology to improve efficiency.
- 88% understood organizational goals.

Finance and insurance results were materially weaker:

- reward: 41%;
- growth: 60%;
- employee voice: 65%;
- collaboration: 68%;
- retention: 73%;
- manager support: 74%;
- work environment and grievance process: 65%;
- training: 63%.

Compared with sector benchmarks, reward was 20 points lower, voice 19 points
lower, regular feedback 17 points lower, growth 15 points lower, and
collaboration 13 points lower.

Information and communication also showed weak reward, growth, voice,
collaboration, and flexibility relative to sector benchmarks.

**Need:** continuous feedback commitments, visible follow-up, recognition,
career actions, safe employee voice, and restricted grievance handling.

## 3. Gender, Care, Advancement, and Psychological Safety

Business Mauritius and Kantar reported:

- female workforce participation around 41%;
- women aged 25 to 59 spent about 5.3 hours per day on household and care work,
  compared with 1.8 hours for men;
- 30% of young parents reported daycare problems;
- women held about 6% of chair roles and 13% of board memberships;
- the national pay gap was reported at 25%, and 29% in the tertiary sector;
- two-thirds of women who had left or wanted to leave intended to return within
  two to three years but faced obstacles.

Reported workplace obstacles included unhealthy environments, lack of
cooperation or respect, negative supervisor comments, lack of appreciation,
and unsupported rapid self-learning. Leaders identified flexibility and
work-life balance as important for attracting and retaining talent. Eighty
percent of interviewed companies lacked a gender policy or strategy.

**Need:** confidential reporting, independent routing, evidence preservation,
restricted access, explicit ownership, non-retaliation audit, progression
analysis, and support constraints.

## 4. Skills Mismatch and Internal Mobility

Statistics Mauritius reporting indicates roughly 50,000 tertiary-educated
people were working below their qualification level. Overqualification
increased from about 5.2% in 2014 to 11.7% in 2024, while underqualification
decreased.

An earlier Business Pulse Survey found that 64% of 504 businesses struggled to
find local personnel, 48% identified business expertise or talent as a major
need, and 69% were dissatisfied with targeted assistance.

**Need:** evidence-backed skills, role requirements, development history,
availability, workload, internal opportunities, and demand forecasting.

## 5. Coordination with Local Autonomy

IBL described a shift from isolated pilots to coordinated group-wide execution,
early involvement of key users, stronger operational visibility, and a second
IT maturity assessment to identify capability and governance gaps.

IBL also described a community of data owners for carbon reporting, improved
source accountability and data quality, and avoidance of duplicate emissions
reporting. Operational disruptions at one entity had cascading effects on
other entities.

ER Group described previously fragmented procurement, technology, digital, and
sustainability activity. Following the ENL-Rogers combination, it is
harmonizing systems, shared services, payroll, job evaluation, engagement
practices, mobility, travel, and whistleblowing while allowing local businesses
to retain entrepreneurial authority.

**Need:** shared semantics, source ownership, governance, and dependency
visibility without imposing one identical process on every business unit.

## 6. Slow Transformation and Adoption

CIEL established a group digital and AI roadmap following maturity assessment
and a group forum intended to identify strengths and synergies. It also
reported slow digital-transformation progress in parts of the group despite
multiple initiatives.

IBL emphasized coordinated execution, measurable indicators, early user
involvement, and teams challenging legacy processes.

**Need:** user validation before scale, visible rules, measurable adoption,
local process ownership, and implementation checkpoints tied to operating
outcomes.

## 7. Employee Experience and Customer Outcomes

CIEL reported that Sunlife employee engagement increased from 73% to 83% while
customer satisfaction rose from 92.3% to 94.8%. The report associated employee
engagement with guest satisfaction and retention.

**Need:** connect workforce conditions and actions to service outcomes without
collapsing both into a vague combined score.

## 8. Complaint Resolution and Customer Feedback

MCB identifies workplace transformation, skills shortages, talent retention,
data protection, interconnectedness, and customer experience as material
issues. It centralized and analyzed customer feedback across business lines
and entities.

Only around 70% of complaints were resolved within five days. MCB reviewed
service standards to reduce wait times and redesigned onboarding and credit
scoring for speed and transparency. It also used cross-functional crisis
simulations with remediation assigned and tracked to closure.

SBM reporting similarly describes cross-functional complaint management,
feedback loops between customer experience, business teams, and HR, recurring
issue analysis, quicker resolution, first-contact resolution, and relationship
managers acting as strategic advisers.

**Need:** complaint-to-root-cause links, shared context, service-level tracking,
feedback-to-process actions, and closure evidence.

## 9. Expectations, Agreements, and Communication

Ombudsperson cases show that projections, unclear limits, and poorly
communicated terms create expectations that later become disputes.

**Need:** record what was promised, the governing agreement, material terms,
communication evidence, and changes to expectations.

## 10. Decision Latency

PwC Mauritius reported that nearly two-thirds of chief executives said key
management decisions take a month or more. It also reported expected AI
integration alongside concerns about lower margins and cybersecurity.

**Need:** decision-ready context with source lineage, defined measures,
exceptions, assumptions, and current ownership.

## 11. Public-Sector Silos and Weak Follow-Through

The Mauritius Enterprise Architecture describes more than 145 operational
systems, a need to break silos, interoperability, end-to-end services, and a
"once-only" data principle through InfoHighway.

It also identifies capability gaps in AI, data, integration, service ownership,
monitoring, change management, and stakeholder engagement.

National Audit reporting stated that only 38% of prior recommendations were
fully implemented. It identified fragmented governance, siloed ministries,
weak performance measures, delayed digital projects, and low use of many
online public services.

**Need:** inter-institution ownership, recommendation implementation tracking,
shared definitions, evidence, and service measures.

## 12. Human Service Alongside Automation

Mauritian insurance reporting describes customers wanting digital convenience
without losing a human voice, preferred language, continuous availability, and
personalized assistance.

**Need:** automation should assemble context and propose the next action. The
responsible person should retain control for sensitive or consequential
interactions.

## 13. Security, Privacy, and Relationship Trust

Mauritian reporting on digital payments and a January 2026 data breach shows
that increased digital adoption brings impersonation, phishing, ransomware,
account takeover, and third-party risk.

Recommended controls include employee training, authentication, dual approval,
segregation of duties, fraud analytics, secure remote access, and incident
response.

**Need:** purpose limitation, access control, consent, retention, third-party
dependency records, dual approval, and incident history.

## 14. Social and Anecdotal Sources

Local forums and social posts repeat themes of ignored complaints, favoritism,
poor management, low support, and unresponsive service. These sources are
useful for scenario language but not for estimating prevalence. The product
roadmap therefore relies on surveys, official statistics, audits, regulator
cases, and company reports for prioritization.

## Overall Conclusion

The core need is a reliable shared history of:

- relationships;
- events and evidence;
- agreements and expectations;
- ownership and dependencies;
- required actions and deadlines;
- decisions and approvals;
- communication and closure;
- measured outcomes.

Customer, employee, supplier, partner, regulator, shareholder, and subsidiary
use cases are different views and policies over that shared foundation.
